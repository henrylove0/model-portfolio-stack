02 02 — Why it’s free

Free stops working only if you step on two specific rakes.

Hosting a photo-heavy site costs money on most platforms because they meter the two heaviest things — image processing and bandwidth. This stack is arranged so that neither one is ever metered.

Trap one · the optimizer cap

If a site builder “optimizes” your photos for you, the free plan typically includes about 1,000 images a month. Your homepage slideshow alone could be twenty. Thirteen visitors and you’re done.

This stack does the opposite: your photos are prepared once, beautifully, before they’re ever uploaded — so no optimizer ever touches them. There is nothing to count.

Trap two · the bandwidth cap

Free front-ends usually meter data — around 100 GB a month. If your photos travelled through that front-end, a hundred high-quality images (≈ 300 MB per gallery pass) would exhaust it after roughly 350 visitors.

Here the photos never pass through it. They’re delivered by Cloudflare’s global network directly, where egress costs nothing.

What the free plans allow, and what this site uses
Vercel · site code100 GB/mo — one visitor downloads ≈ 65 KB → about 1.6M visitors
Vercel · processing125k operations — visitors use ~0; only your admin logins
Cloudflare R2 · storage10 GB free — 100 photos ≈ 0.3 GB
Cloudflare R2 · deliveryegress is always free; edge cache absorbs repeat views
The one sentence version

Your code comes from Vercel; your photographs come from Cloudflare. Each platform meters only the thing that’s tiny, and gives away the thing that’s heavy.

03 03 — How it works

Two specialists, one seamless site.

When someone opens your site, their browser quietly asks two different companies for two different kinds of thing — and stitches them into one experience.

Your visitor
the page itself — text, layout, the slideshow engine. Tiny: about the size of one photograph’s thumbnail.
Vercel
≈ 65 KB — could be served millions of times inside the free plan
Your visitor
the photographs — your 4K WebP files, delivered from the data center nearest to each visitor. Never counted against the site.
Cloudflare R2
≈ 2–3.5 MB each — free egress, cached at the edge worldwide
Three quiet ideas underneath
The manifest

One small file in the cloud is the single source of truth: which photos belong to which slideshow. Your admin rewrites it; the website reads it. No database, no rebuild, no waiting on a developer.

Prepared photos

Before upload, every photo is resized to a maximum of 3840 pixels (true 4K) and encoded as WebP at high quality — about 1.5–3.5 MB instead of 35 MB, indistinguishable on screen. The heavy original never travels.

A locked door

The admin sits behind a password of your choosing (stored as an irreversible fingerprint, never as text). Sessions expire; brute-force attempts are throttled. Only you rearrange your book.

04 04 — Running your site

The admin: your book, on your schedule.

Everything on the site is movable with a drag. Open admin.yourname.com on any device — there is no app to install and nothing to learn beyond dragging.

Sign in

One password. Your session remembers you for a week on each device.

Choose a shelf

A sidebar lists Home Slideshow, About Photo, and every project — up to twenty. Pick one.

Drop photos — one or twenty at once

Drag files straight from your desktop, or click to browse. Multiple files queue automatically, with progress for each.

Arrange by dragging

Drag any photo onto another slot to reorder. Drop on the dashed box to send it to the end. Small arrows nudge by one.

Move between galleries

Every photo carries a “Move to…” control — send it from Home to Project 07 in one click. Retitle any project inline.

That’s it — it saves itself

Every change writes itself and is live worldwide within about a minute. There is no publish button to forget.

Golden rule

Always add photos through the admin — never into cloud storage directly. The admin is what tells the website the photo exists. Files dropped into storage alone would sit there unseen.

05 05 — Your photos

Shoot huge. The stack handles the rest.

Camera originals of any size — including 35 MB, 6000×9000 medium-format files — are exactly what this was built around. Nothing about your workflow needs to shrink.

Sharp on a phone, exact on a 4K monitor. Conversion happens in your browser the moment you drop the file — originals never leave your computer.

06 06 — Before launch

A ten-minute checklist before you share the link.

Most of this is done once, by whoever sets the site up for you. Keep this page for the day you take the keys.

Lock the admin

Remove “open access” testing mode so the password is required again. Two clicks in the hosting dashboard and one redeploy.

Connect your domain

yourname.com and admin.yourname.com, verified in the hosting dashboard. HTTPS is automatic.

Make it yours

Real Instagram handle, real email, agency names and regions, your story and measurements. Each is one short text edit.

The single-photo test

Drop one photo into the home slideshow. Within a minute it should appear on the live site — that proves the whole pipeline end to end.

The zero-bytes test

Open the site with the browser’s developer tools → Network tab. Reload and click through: every photograph’s address should show your Cloudflare domain — never the hosting one. If any photo loads from the host, something is proxying and the free plan is bleeding.

Phone check

Swipe the slideshow, open every gallery, tap every menu. The site is fully responsive — confirm it feels like you.

07 07 — When you blow up

What happens if the internet falls in love with you.

A press feature, a viral campaign, a million visits in a week — the stack absorbs far more than you’ll realistically hit. Here is the honest ceiling map.

The first ceilingVercel’s 100 GB — at roughly 1.6M monthly visitors. The dashboard shows usage; you’d see it coming weeks out.
Photoseffectively uncapped — Cloudflare doesn’t meter their bandwidth at all
Storage10 GB ≈ 3,000 photos; years of galleries
If you hit itthe host pauses the site until the month resets — it emails you first; nothing is lost
The fix, when neededone paid plan (~$20/mo) multiplies the ceiling ~10×; everything else stays free
If the site changes hands

Everything transfers cleanly: the code lives in a git repository, the photos in your own cloud bucket, and both platforms invite the new owner via email. An afternoon, with zero downtime.

If you want changes

Typography, palette, page structure, number of galleries — the codebase is small, commented, and built on the most common tools in the industry. Any competent developer can run with it.

The promise

Between here and a million visitors a month, the budget does not move. You will outgrow agencies, cameras and calendars long before you outgrow this bill.
